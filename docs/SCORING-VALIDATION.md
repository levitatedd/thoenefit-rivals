# Scoring validation with simulated competitions (#14)

Generated by `npm run simulate:report` (apps/mobile) from `src/features/scoring/simulation`. Every number below comes from the real v1 scoring engine (#2) applied to **synthetic, seeded** activity — no real people, devices or health data. 400 simulated competitions per scenario; re-running produces exactly the same report. No scoring rules were changed for this analysis.

## Set-up

- Rules: `v1` — daily points = min(200, 100 × activity ÷ personal goal), summed over days; 0.01-point precision; ties share a place.
- Each simulated day is a platform daily total drawn around the player’s typical day with ±30% day-to-day variation (log-normal).
- Fitness levels (typical day):
  - **Light**: 5,000 steps / 250 active kcal
  - **Moderate**: 8,500 steps / 450 active kcal
  - **Active**: 12,000 steps / 650 active kcal
  - **Athlete**: 17,000 steps / 950 active kcal
- "Goal factor" = personal goal ÷ the player’s typical day (1.0 = honest, 0.5 = half their usual day).
- Fairness advice uses each player’s synthetic 28-day history and the configured bands: < 60% potentially easy, < 80% below usual, ≤ 120% near usual, above that above usual.
- "Win share" counts a shared first place as a fraction of a win, so shares add up to 100%.

## 1. Personal goals level different fitness levels

Four players (light, moderate, active, athlete), everyone with an honest goal. A perfectly fair contest gives each 25.0%.

| Win share | 1-day (personal goals) | 3-day (personal goals) | 7-day (personal goals) | 7-day (one shared goal, ≈ raw totals) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Light | 22.0% | 27.0% | 28.7% | 0.0% |
| Moderate | 31.6% | 26.5% | 21.3% | 0.0% |
| Active | 27.8% | 23.5% | 24.3% | 1.8% |
| Athlete | 18.6% | 23.0% | 25.8% | 98.3% |

Calorie Clash behaves the same (7-day win shares: 22.1% / 25.8% / 25.4% / 26.8%).

**Finding:** goal-relative scoring works as intended — a light walker competes on equal terms with an athlete. Ranked by raw activity, the athlete would win almost every time. 1-day competitions are noisier (more luck), as expected.

## 2. Goal ambition: easy, honest and ambitious goals

One moderate player sets a goal at the given factor of their typical day; three moderate peers are honest. Fair share = 25.0%.

| Goal factor | Advice shown privately (most common) | Win share 1-day | Win share 3-day | Win share 7-day | Days at cap (7-day) | Future-goal suggestion (7-day) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0.5 | potentially-easy (97.5%) | 88.4% | 99.0% | 100.0% | 42.3% | 64.3% |
| 0.7 | below-usual (88.8%) | 63.2% | 87.5% | 96.5% | 9.8% | 1.8% |
| 0.85 | near-usual (92.0%) | 39.8% | 56.3% | 72.3% | 2.6% | 0.0% |
| 1 | near-usual (97.0%) | 29.5% | 27.3% | 24.0% | 0.6% | 0.0% |
| 1.3 | above-usual (95.5%) | 8.0% | 4.3% | 1.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |

**Findings:**

- **Easy goals dominate.** At half their usual day a player wins 88.4% of 1-day and 100.0% of 7-day competitions against honest peers. The private warning fires ("potentially easy"), but by design it never affects points, so it does not change the outcome. The 200-point cap limits the advantage per day but does not neutralise it.
- **The "near usual" band (80–120%) is wide.** A goal at 85% of a typical day is labelled *near usual* in most runs, yet wins 72.3% of 7-day competitions — about 2.9× a fair share.
- **Ambitious goals are punished.** At 130% a player wins only 1.0% of 7-day competitions, while the advice reads like praise ("Nice ambition!").
- **The future-goal suggestion** (≥ 3 capped days in 7) catches very easy goals (64.3% of 7-day competitions at 0.5) but almost never moderately easy ones (1.8% at 0.7).
- Longer competitions amplify any goal advantage: small daily edges add up over 7 days.

## 3. Missed days and comeback potential

One moderate player has confirmed zero days at the start of the competition; three honest moderate peers. Fair share = 25.0%.

| Win share | 3-day | 7-day |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| No missed days | 25.3% | 26.0% |
| 1 missed day | 1.0% | 5.5% |
| 1 missed day + one 2× day | 13.3% | 21.5% |
| 1 missed day + one 3× day (capped) | 20.8% | 24.3% |
| 2 missed days | 0.0% | 0.3% |

Worked example (goal 10,000 steps, 7 days; exact, not random):

| Player | Daily steps | Daily points | Total | Rank |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| comeback | 0, 0, 25,000, 25,000, 20,000, 15,000, 12,000 | 0, 0, 200, 200, 200, 150, 120 | 870 | 1 |
| steady | 12,000, 12,000, 12,000, 12,000, 12,000, 12,000, 12,000 | 120, 120, 120, 120, 120, 120, 120 | 840 | 2 |
| spike | 6,000, 6,000, 60,000, 6,000, 6,000, 6,000, 6,000 | 60, 60, 200, 60, 60, 60, 60 | 560 | 3 |

**Findings:**

- **Comebacks are possible but bounded.** A missed day costs about one typical day (~100 points); one exceptional day can earn at most 100 points more than a typical day (the cap), so one big day can make up for one missed day, but not for two.
- **The cap works as intended.** The "spike" player’s 60,000-step day would be worth 600 points uncapped and win the week; capped at 200 it can’t outweigh consistent effort.
- Short competitions are unforgiving: missing one day of three is very hard to recover from.

## 4. Ties

Four moderate players, all with the same goal factor. Share of competitions where first place is shared:

| Everyone’s goal factor | 1-day | 3-day | 7-day |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 0.7 | 5.3% | 0.0% | 0.0% |
| 0.5 | 60.0% | 1.5% | 0.0% |
| 0.4 | 96.5% | 48.0% | 3.8% |

**Findings:** with realistic goals, exact ties for first are rare thanks to 0.01-point precision. When goals are very easy, players hit the 200-point cap together and **short competitions frequently end in ties** (by design, ties stay ties).

## Recommendations for owner review

Nothing below has been implemented. The scoring formula and thresholds are unchanged pending your decision.

1. **Keep the core formula.** Goal-relative points with a per-day cap levels fitness differences, rewards consistency, limits outlier days and still allows comebacks.
2. **Easy goals are the main fairness risk.** Options (choose any, or none for a trusted-friends v1):
   - a. Show opponents a neutral goal indicator (e.g. "goal set below usual") with the owner’s opt-in, as #2 already allows.
   - b. Let competition creators require a minimum goal relative to each player’s own baseline (e.g. ≥ 80%) — advisory today, optional rule later.
   - c. Narrow the "near usual" band (currently 80–120%), e.g. to 90–110%, so modestly easy goals are flagged.
3. **Reword the above-usual advice** so it also explains the trade-off ("a higher goal makes each point harder to earn"), since ambitious goals strongly reduce the chance of winning.
4. **Ties in short competitions:** keep shared places (as agreed), and have the leaderboard (#3) explain ties clearly; optionally show raw activity as context without using it to rank.
5. **Validate with real data later:** these simulations assume ±30% daily variation for everyone; real variability differs by person and device. Re-run with real (consented) aggregates when health integrations exist.

Owner decision on these options, with extra simulations (goals at 80–95% of a usual day): [PRIVACY-SECURITY.md → Easy goals](PRIVACY-SECURITY.md#5-easy-goals-options-for-a-decision) (#10).

## Limitations

- Synthetic data: invented fitness profiles and a simple day-to-day variation model; no weekly patterns, illness streaks or device gaps beyond the modelled missed days.
- Only platform daily totals are simulated, so eligibility edge cases (overlaps, held records) are covered by the unit tests in #2, not here.
- Win shares are estimates from 400 runs (± about 2–4 percentage points).
